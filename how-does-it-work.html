From moment to consequence.

CPS connects what an artificial character can perceive, what it knows, what it decides, what actually happens, and what that changes later.

CPS is built for artificial characters that continue to exist in time. Their conversations, actions, relationships, commitments and memories become part of an evolving world rather than disappearing when an interaction ends.

Language does not automatically become reality.

A language model can interpret a situation, propose an action or generate dialogue. That does not make the described event true.

If an Actor says “I’ll go,” CPS does not treat that sentence as evidence that they moved. A decision, a commitment, scheduled execution, movement, an encounter and its outcome are distinct states.

CPS keeps those states separate so later behavior can depend on what actually happened rather than what a model merely said happened.

Four practical questions

What is true — and who knows it?

CPS separates world truth from what individual Actors know, believe, suspect or remember. Information remains Actor-local and retains its source, confidence and provenance, allowing direct observation, testimony and uncertain belief to remain meaningfully different.

What can happen next?

Possibility is constrained by the current world: time, location, activity, schedule, existing commitments, relationships, accessible information and other relevant context. CPS determines which opportunities are legitimate rather than allowing a model to freely invent what happened next.

What does the Actor decide?

An opportunity does not automatically become an action. An Actor can respond, refuse, defer, continue an existing commitment or do nothing. When a decision requires future execution, CPS can retain it as durable state rather than relying on conversational prose to remember it.

What actually happened?

CPS keeps intention separate from execution. Movement must occur in the world. Encounters must actually take place. Actions can succeed, fail or remain incomplete. Only legitimate outcomes become evidence that later memory, relationships and behavior can depend on.

From context to consequence.

A persistent artificial life is not one continuous decision loop. Most things do not become actions. CPS preserves the intermediate states so that consequences occur only when the required causal steps actually happened.

Context / observation

Something becomes available to an Actor through the world, communication, memory or another legitimate source. Availability does not guarantee that the Actor notices it or acts on it.

Relevance / decision

Accessible context may become relevant to the Actor’s current situation. The result can be an action, a deferred intention, continued existing behavior or no response at all.

Commitment / execution

When something must happen later, CPS can preserve the obligation beyond the conversation that created it. Scheduling and world execution determine whether the intended action is actually attempted.

Outcome / consequence

Completed actions create canonical evidence. Outcomes may change what Actors know, remember, believe, how relationships evolve, where Actors are, what commitments remain, and what later situations become possible.

Information can spread. It doesn’t have to.

Knowledge in CPS is not automatically global.

When one Actor learns something from another, the recipient receives their own epistemic record rather than inheriting the source Actor’s experience as objective truth. CPS can retain who supplied the information, whether it was directly observed or reported, and how it relates to earlier evidence.

Whether that information becomes relevant again depends on later circumstances. It may influence another exchange, remain private or irrelevant, be contradicted by new evidence, or simply never matter again.

Propagation is therefore a consequence of legitimate social circumstances, not the default destination of every memory.

CPS Studio is the proof surface

CPS Studio makes causal claims inspectable.

An operator can trace what an Actor had access to, what became relevant, what was interpreted, what was decided, which obligations existed, what was scheduled, what actually executed, and what canonical state changed afterward.

Speech, intention, schedule and outcome remain distinguishable.

This makes it possible to inspect not only what the system produced, but why later behavior is allowed to depend on it.

Language models interpret.
CPS preserves causality.

A model may interpret context, propose a response or generate dialogue. CPS remains responsible for the structured state surrounding that output: world facts, Actor-local knowledge, decisions, commitments, timing, movement, encounters and their resulting evidence.

A language model can describe an event.

CPS determines whether that event actually became part of the world.
