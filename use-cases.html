Where CPS can be used

Persistence, privacy, and propagation in practice.

Section 01

The core pattern

A conversation does not end when the message is spoken. In CPS, information can become structured memory. That memory can create a signal, become socially relevant, find an opportunity to move, pass or fail privacy and relationship gates, and become a new memory in another actor.

Moment → Memory → Signal → Opportunity → Exchange → Propagated Memory → Consequence

CPS Studio makes the path inspectable: who knew it, where it came from, how it changed, and why it moved or stopped.

Section 02

Primary use cases

These are the strongest current CPS use cases because they depend directly on propagation, bounded access, partial memory, mutation, and lineage.

01

Rumor Mutation Lab

Watch one message change as it moves through different actors.

A controlled demonstrator where one piece of information enters a small group and changes through trust, fear, authority, personality, or misunderstanding.

Example:

The player tells one actor: “The archive door will be open tonight.” One actor sees opportunity. Another sees danger. A third treats it as institutional risk. CPS shows each version and preserves the chain.

What CPS proves:

  • propagation
  • mutation
  • actor-specific interpretation
  • lineage
  • source attribution

02

Belief Change Observatory

Study how trust, evidence, authority, and hearsay shift what agents believe.

A research-facing scenario for observing how belief changes over time inside a bounded artificial group. Different actors may accept, doubt, reinterpret, or ignore the same claim depending on who said it and how it reached them.

Example:

A weak claim becomes more credible after passing through a trusted actor. CPS shows who accepted it, who doubted it, and how confidence changed.

What CPS proves:

  • belief movement
  • confidence change
  • trust-mediated updates
  • bounded access
  • provenance

03

Social Consequence Worlds

Build interactive worlds where characters remember, retell, withhold, and react.

CPS can support authored game-like worlds where social memory creates consequence. Characters do not all share the same knowledge. They remember different versions, hear things from different sources, and react later based on what reached them.

Example:

The player privately helps one character but lies to another. A third character hears a distorted version later. The player is confronted with a fragment they did not expect to return.

What CPS proves:

  • local memory
  • asymmetric knowledge
  • delayed consequence
  • reputation without a global reputation meter
  • social recall

04

Communication Rehearsal

Test how messages move through teams, roles, and institutions.

A scenario format for exploring how communication changes as it moves through a group. A manager, employee, observer, or outsider may each interpret the same message differently based on role, trust, pressure, and prior context.

Example:

A leader says, “I’m concerned about the direction of the project.” One actor hears warning. Another hears criticism. Someone else feels excluded. CPS shows where the interpretation broke down.

What CPS proves:

  • message clarity
  • role pressure
  • misunderstanding
  • authority effects
  • downstream interpretation

05

Mediation and Negotiation Simulator

Explore how disclosure order, trust, and sequencing shape conflict or agreement.

A scenario where the player tries to resolve tension between actors by deciding whom to speak to first, what to reveal, what to withhold, and when to correct a distorted claim.

Example:

One actor trusts Clara but distrusts Gregory. Telling Gregory first escalates the conflict. Telling Clara first creates a safer path for the same information to reach the group.

What CPS proves:

  • sequencing effects
  • relationship topology
  • trust gates
  • repair paths
  • conflict escalation or resolution

06

Privacy Boundary Testbed

Inspect what spreads, what stays private, what becomes hearsay, and what gets blocked.

A testbed for systems where not every agent should know everything. CPS can show when information is allowed to propagate, when it is blocked, when it becomes hearsay, and when a sensitive detail remains contained.

Example:

The player shares private information with one actor. CPS allows internal recall but blocks wider propagation because the relationship and sensitivity gates do not permit transmission.

What CPS proves:

  • privacy gates
  • sensitive information handling
  • bounded memory
  • blocked propagation
  • containment logic

07

Museum Memory Installation

Let visitor interactions leave traces that later visitors encounter indirectly.

A public installation where visitors speak to or influence artificial actors. Instead of resetting after every interaction, selected traces remain inside the artificial community and may later reappear through another actor.

Example:

A visitor leaves a message with one character. Hours later, another visitor encounters a changed version of that message through a different character, with the system able to show how it travelled.

What CPS proves:

  • visitor trace continuity
  • public-facing propagation
  • memory transformation
  • accessible explanation
  • social afterlife of interaction

08

Narrative Continuity Tool

Track who knows what inside a fictional group.

A tool for writers, worldbuilders, and narrative designers who need characters to hold different knowledge states. CPS can help track what each actor knows, what they heard directly, what they heard second-hand, and how that knowledge changes the story.

Example:

A secret, a rumor, and a promise exist in the same fictional world. Different characters know different fragments. CPS helps preserve continuity without flattening everyone into one shared knowledge state.

What CPS proves:

  • character-specific knowledge
  • story continuity
  • rumor tracking
  • contradictory memory
  • author inspection

Section 03

Experimental directions

These use cases extend the same mechanism into education, civic scenarios, player research, and future multi-agent systems.

09

AI Literacy Experience

Show that AI agents have partial memory, bounded access, hearsay, and uncertainty.

An educational experience where people can see why an agent believes something, where the information came from, and why another agent may not know the same thing.

10

Civic Framing Scenario Lab

Observe how public messages are reframed inside a small simulated community.

A bounded scenario for studying how proposals, accusations, reforms, or public statements change as they move through trust, authority, skepticism, fear, and group identity.

11

Player Behavior Analytics

Study how players disclose, correct, manipulate, or repair information inside social systems.

A future-facing layer where structured session data can reveal how players interact with information flow: whom they trust, what they reveal, when they intervene, and how they respond to mutation.

12

Inspectable AI Council

A future multi-agent assistant where advice comes with a visible decision trail.

Several specialized agents hold partial context, discuss a user situation, and produce one recommendation with an inspectable record of what each agent knew, shared, challenged, or changed.

Section 04

How to read these use cases

Every use case is a variation of the same CPS question: What happens after information enters a social system?

  • who knows something
  • where it came from
  • whether it is direct knowledge or hearsay
  • whether it can move
  • how it changed
  • why it was blocked
  • what it caused later
